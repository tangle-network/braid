I’ll verify current Braid and upstream owner contracts, compare the strongest substitutes, then narrow the thesis to a falsifiable experiment while separating evidence from unsupported claims.

The decisive test is whether profile evolution beats corrected memory and durable context without rewarding needless revisions. Upstream now rejects unsupported lifecycle signals, but still lacks pause/resume control.

## Executive decision: **NARROW, then pursue conditionally**

One evolving root `AgentProfile` is coherent **as the user-controlled definition of behavior**, not as the container for an entire enduring agent.

The strongest product is: **one place to establish what matters, authorize bounded work, correct misunderstandings, and recover the thread later—without becoming an agent-system operator.** The root may change its methods and author capabilities, but those changes must earn their complexity.

The strongest counterargument is that a good native harness, editable memory, saved workflows, and ordinary task records may deliver nearly all the value with fewer abstractions. Braid could become an expensive configuration layer that manufactures objectives and asks users to supervise its self-improvement.

**Recommendation:** test a persistent work assistant for a narrow initial audience. Do not launch a universal personal agent, autonomous research organization, or specialist marketplace. Continue only if Braid reduces total user effort and preserves control better than the strongest simpler substitute.

## 1. What should endure?

### The strongest thesis

The root provides a **stable point of user direction across changing work and execution systems**. Its canonical profile defines how it discovers possible objectives, distinguishes conversation from action, selects methods, authors child profiles, and proposes revisions.

That is more useful than a persistent persona. The valuable continuity is: “You understand what I authorized, what remains unresolved, and how I corrected you.”

But the durable product unit is a **combination with separate owners**:

| Component | What it contains | Ownership boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Canonical `AgentProfile` | Behavioral instructions, objective-discovery method, capability references, child-authoring method | User-controlled content under Interface’s schema |
| Relationship record | Conversations, explicit preferences, tentative interpretations, corrections | User-scoped records; Braid owns its conversation graph |
| Commitments | Accepted objectives, constraints, acceptance conditions, continuation permissions | User decisions linked to Runtime’s admitted execution |
| Capability library | Procedures, code, skills, exact child profiles, validation evidence | Individually versioned artifacts; ownership and licensing remain explicit |
| Execution and authority | Jobs, reservations, grants, sessions, effects, recovery state | Runtime, providers, Cloud, and integration owners |

This preserves **one agent configuration**. Task state and an account’s authorization are not competing configurations. A profile may request a capability; it cannot grant itself access by editing its permissions text. Braid already separates profile, connection, conversation, run, and provider ownership. 

### The strongest objection

“One evolving profile” becomes misleading when every correction changes global behavior. A preference for terse operational updates may be inappropriate during personal discussion. A successful consulting procedure may be unsuitable for another client. The root risks accumulating contradictory instructions while presenting that accumulation as learning.

Therefore, **prefer scoped knowledge and reusable methods over global profile mutation**. Revise the root when the lesson genuinely concerns how it should operate across relevant future situations.

The user should be able to inspect, correct, export, and fork the whole continuity package—not merely its prompt. Exports should separate profiles, private knowledge, commitments, artifacts, receipts, and unavailable dependencies. Credentials should require rebinding.

Forks should start with **inactive continuing commitments** unless explicitly reauthorized; copying a relationship must not duplicate external obligations.

Deletion must remove derived memories and summaries as well as original text. Retained historical receipts may need redaction or destruction, making some past executions unreconstructable. Say that plainly. “Immutable provenance” must not become an excuse to retain personal content indefinitely, and deletion cannot undo messages or other effects already delivered externally.

## 2. The first month: what the user should experience

The following is a **proposed journey**, not current Braid behavior.

**First conversation: no objective required.** A consultant arrives saying, “This engagement feels scattered. I don’t know what I need.”

The root asks one discriminating question: “Is the immediate difficulty deciding what to do, or having space to explain what is happening?” It does not generate a project plan automatically.

The user explains and concludes, “Actually, I just needed to say it aloud.” The useful result is an accurate reflection and clarification. No child, objective, reminder, or personality inference is created. There is no unfinished task merely because no artifact exists.

**A later conversation: discover bounded work.** The consultant supplies kickoff notes. The root notices that the notes contain incompatible expectations and proposes: “The next useful result may be a one-page list of decisions needed before Monday—not another research report.”

The proposal states its evidence, expected output, limits, and alternatives, including doing nothing. The user agrees to private drafting from those notes, with no client contact.

**During longer work: conversation remains available.** The root delegates source extraction to an exact, read-only child profile where useful. Another part may be handled directly. The user asks, “What have you found?” and receives the latest acknowledged state—not a speculative progress narrative.

Then the user says, “Drop the vendor comparison.” The system records the change immediately, explains which work it has stopped admitting, and separately reports whether running work acknowledged cancellation. A late vendor result remains historical evidence; it cannot restore the abandoned objective.

**After interruption: reconstruct, do not merely resume chat.** Returning three days later, the user sees:

> The decision brief is drafted but not accepted. The vendor branch was cancelled. One source remains unavailable. No client messages were sent. The next step is your review of two unresolved assumptions.

The user should not reread hundreds of tool events to understand this.

**Week two: an understandable revision.** After repeated corrections, the root proposes: “For this client, separate confirmed commitments from inferred requests.” The preview includes the corrections supporting it, its scope, a behavioral example, and the exact profile or method change. It is labeled unmeasured until tested.

**Month end: continuity without obligatory evolution.** The user can review active commitments, rejected proposals, corrections, method changes, and resources consumed. A second user starting from the same seed may develop a deterministic checklist and no recurring child organization.

**Different structures are permitted, not required.** Identical structures can also be correct. The test is suitability, not visible divergence.

Current Braid offers useful foundations—explicit feedback, profile-draft review, separate analysis, and provenance-aware forks—but the supplied journey extends beyond those inspected mechanisms.  

## 3. Seeds should establish a starting contract, not an identity

Start with one restrained default. Optional seeds should describe **working approaches**, such as evidence-first investigation or conservative recurring administration—not claim to be an agronomist, therapist, or investment professional.

A seed registry should disclose dependencies, requested access, supported execution paths, evaluation scope, version, and maintenance status. A template is not a qualified service. Registry updates should produce reviewable changes, never silently alter active users.

Braid’s existing contract already rejects inventing a separate remote Braid catalog for its first release. Preserve that boundary initially. 

The first user-facing configuration should concern access, retention, and resource limits—not orchestration topology. Ask additional questions only when their answers change a consequential decision.

Learn lightweight, reversible presentation preferences from interaction. Ask explicitly about persistent obligations, proactive contact, sensitive retention, external actions, and authority expansion.

Tentative interpretations need source, scope, uncertainty, and expiry. Repetition in the agent’s own summaries must not become independent corroboration. “You seemed rushed today” must not become “Your personality is impatient.”

The default update policy should allow easy correction while requiring explicit activation of material changes to objective discovery, delegation strategy, or ongoing behavior. That policy itself cannot be broadened by a proposed profile edit.

## 4. General mechanics do not imply general competence

These are product-boundary examples, not domain advice.

| Case | Useful common mechanism | Distinct validation and authority requirement |
|---|---|---|
| **Consulting engagement** | Turn scattered material into proposed deliverables, unresolved questions, and reviewable drafts | Client-specific confidentiality, source traceability, scope acceptance, and explicit authority to communicate externally |
| **Continuing small-business obligation** | Track an agreed recurring obligation and surface exceptions | Authoritative business records, reliable scheduling, reconciliation, escalation ownership, and evidence of completion rather than “the agent ran” |
| **Farming decision dependent on observations** | Organize observations, expose missing inputs, prepare questions for a responsible practitioner | Observation location, age, quality, and relevance; locally appropriate expertise; no inference that a sensor reading or inspection occurred |
| **Financial research** | Produce an as-of evidence pack with reproducible calculations and competing interpretations | Point-in-time data, revision history, source licensing and freshness, qualified review where needed; research access must not imply trading authority |
| **Continuing personal support** | Listen, clarify, remember user-approved preferences, and revisit a user-requested topic | Sensitive-data boundaries, no diagnostic authority, no fabricated human relationship, no optimization for engagement or dependency |

The common structure is **intent, evidence, permission, bounded action or waiting, review, and correction**. The success conditions differ fundamentally.

A financial report may permit reproducible numerical checks. A farming question may remain blocked pending an external observation. A support conversation may be complete because the user feels accurately understood—not because a score increased.

A domain-specific qualification, instrument, inspection, or professional relationship is an external prerequisite. A root can recognize or request it; authoring another profile cannot manufacture it.

This also means validation should sometimes come from outside the root’s descendants. Having several similar models agree is not a substitute for independent evidence or accountable expertise.

## 5. The substitutes are already strong

Claude documents background scripted workflows with a responsive conversation, reusable orchestration, and session-associated recovery. Its documented workflow limitations include no mid-run user input into the workflow and replay that can rerun later agents. Codex documents persistent, user-controlled goals with completion conditions and lifecycle controls. These are substantial substitutes, though not proof of equivalent cross-host topology semantics. 

Claude also offers editable instructions and automatic memory based on corrections and preferences. Consequently, “it remembers you” is not a sufficient differentiator. 

### Tradeoff table

The judgments below are architectural assessments, not measured product comparisons.

| Substitute | Switching cost and attention | Reliability and verifiable value | What Braid must beat |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Strong native harness + workflows + memory** | Lowest additional setup for existing users; preserves native behavior | Useful task completion without translating every feature through another client | Less re-explanation, easier correction and recovery—not merely runner choice |
| **Managed long-running framework** | More application engineering; less need to construct remote-worker plumbing | Deep Agents documents independently running workers, persistent task metadata, updates, and cancellation through Agent Protocol deployments | An end-user product that reduces supervisory work, not another framework wrapper |
| **Domain software + automation** | Existing records and organizational habits remain authoritative | Explicit business processes can be easier to validate; Power Automate already connects approvals to business systems | Better handling of ambiguous needs and exceptions without duplicating the system of record |
| **No automation** | No setup or agent supervision; human effort remains | Often preferable for infrequent, sensitive, poorly observable, or rapidly changing work | Net benefit after onboarding, review, correction, maintenance, and failures |

The documented Deep Agents update operation interrupts a run and starts another on the same thread; that should not be conflated with arbitrary process-state steering. Microsoft’s approval documentation likewise distinguishes persisted business decisions from notification and flow execution. 

Pi provides extensibility, while Pi Durable describes checkpointed tasks, retry-aware tools, ownership trees, and durable application documents. These increase the burden of justification for building another execution mechanism; they do not establish Braid adapter support. 

**Strongest substitute:** the user’s best native harness, properly configured.

Future models may reduce cross-harness routing’s value. Treat portability as an exit and continuity property, not the core economic thesis. If Braid cannot win while holding the runner constant, runner proliferation is unlikely to rescue it.

## 6. The lifecycle proposal is necessary—but sequencing and ownership matter

The lifecycle document describes real prerequisites for dependable continuing work. It should be an acceptance boundary, not an invitation to build every possible scheduling abstraction.

**Important upstream change:** the inspected Runtime head, `849144e`, now exposes cancel-only `RootSignal`; pause/resume/ask were removed rather than implemented. Braid’s pinned contract therefore must not be interpreted as possessing those controls.  

### Reuse owners before adding mechanisms

Runtime’s maintained inventory names supervised-run journals, fenced SQL ownership, retained admissions and recovery. It identifies Platform workflow suspensions as the existing sleeping wait/timer owner, and Cloud as workspace-recovery owner. These are source-documented contracts, not integration tests performed here. 

The composition should therefore be:

- **Runtime:** execution admission, exact identities, recursive reservations, coordination, fencing, and recovery.
- **Existing Platform waits/queue:** durable sleeping and delivery, exposed through the shared execution composition rather than a Braid scheduler.
- **Braid:** user decisions, conversation history, control requests, and acknowledged projections.
- **Cloud/providers/integrations:** actual process, workspace, and external-effect operations.

Discovery’s architecture expressly rejects a separate universal coordinator. Reuse its recursive profile/task approach, not a new Discovery service. 

### The consequential guarantees

**Inbox and wakeup.** A committed child result must make unfinished consumption discoverable. Notifications are hints. Use the owning transaction or recoverable outbox/reconciliation path to close the result–wakeup gap. Delivery may repeat; admission of the same continuation must not. Persist consumed positions and operation identities, not only messages.

**Responsive root and conflicting events.** Reserve foreground capacity so a long worker or synthesis does not monopolize interaction. Parallel reasoning may generate proposals, but only a current fenced owner commits decisions. Define ordering through durable revisions, not wall-clock guesses. Once steering or revocation is acknowledged, later admission must check it; an older worker result cannot overwrite it.

**Exact subtree control.** Fence new descendant admission at the control boundary, then reconcile existing descendants individually. “Requested,” “suspended,” “unsupported,” and “unknown” are different outcomes. Resolve destructive controls to exact IDs: Braid’s current helper accepts the first matching worker label as well as IDs, so ambiguous labels should not become an exact-subtree control surface. 

**Resources.** One authoritative reservation owner must conserve the parent allowance through all descendants. Include root reasoning, verification, retries, tools, infrastructure, and shutdown. Unknown usage stays unknown; missing acknowledgements do not release reservations. Expose enforceable token/call/concurrency/deadline bounds separately from estimated dollars.

Do not assume optimization machinery supplies this guarantee: the pursuit-version contract documents zero or estimated reservation fallback when a per-version budget is absent. That is not evidence of an enforceable hard currency cap. 

**Recovery.** Restore decisions and reconcile original worker bindings before replacement. A checkpoint is a recorded recovery boundary, not an atomic copy of all machines or a rollback of external effects. Expired workspaces, lost native sessions, unresolved effects, pending approvals, and obsolete profile activations must remain visible.

**Completion.** Separate process termination, artifact delivery, artifact acceptance, and objective completion. Current scored pursuit-version search should remain one optional method—not the default mechanism for personal support or objective discovery. Runtime’s improvement documentation also explicitly separates evaluation from activation authority.  

### What earns the durability claim

Require deterministic fault assertions for uncertain admission, lost wakes, duplicate/reordered events, two recovering coordinators, steering races, cancellation versus grandchild creation, concurrent reservation exhaustion, stale approvals, revoked timers, missing hosts, expired checkpoints, and invalid successful artifacts.

Then require the supplied **real-cloud consumer proof through Braid**: two children and a bounded grandchild; client disconnect; offline completion; supported pause/resume; coordinator restart; steering; subtree cancellation; reconnect; artifact acceptance and reconciled accounting. Record exact builds and execution identities. Fake providers cannot replace this.

Set recovery, acknowledgement, backlog, concurrency, and enforceable-resource targets before running that proof. No fleet expansion until it passes.

However, test whether users value objective clarification **before** completing the entire lifecycle program. Desirability experiments can use explicit, manually activated revisions. Autonomous continuing operation must wait for durability.

## 7. The first adoption experiment

**Proposed audience:** twelve terminal-comfortable users—six independent consultants or operations practitioners, six engineering/research practitioners—with recurring work and authority to review the outputs. This is an exploratory cohort, not evidence of existing demand.

**Initial non-coding job:** turn messy engagement notes into the *right next reviewable output*. Sometimes that is a decision brief; sometimes it is one clarifying question; sometimes no work should follow. Use supplied documents and private drafts. No client outreach, purchases, professional advice, or operational changes.

The paired technical job should have an inspectable acceptance surface, such as an evidence-backed investigation. The jobs test a shared mechanism without pretending that success generalizes to farming, finance, or personal care.

### Comparison

Run a proposed four-week, counterbalanced comparison against a well-configured native harness. Match model, accessible sources, tools, resource ceilings, and starting information where possible. Give the native arm the same explicit user corrections; do not cripple its memory to manufacture a win.

Add held-out comparisons between Braid’s original seed and accepted revisions. That distinguishes value from Braid’s interface and execution continuity from value attributable to evolving behavior.

Measure active user minutes—including setup, review, repair, re-explanation, and recovery—alongside independently assessed artifact acceptance, delayed user endorsement of discovered objectives, unwanted work, and control failures.

For listening-only cases, evaluate whether the system accurately understood the user and avoided unwanted action. Do not score them as failed productivity.

**Proposed continuation threshold:** at least 20% lower median active user effort, no lower observed artifact acceptance, no unresolved material authority violation, and at least eight of twelve users choosing continued use at the disclosed ongoing cost. Report individual results and uncertainty; twelve users cannot establish universal usefulness.

Stop an affected run immediately for unauthorized effects, cross-context disclosure, lost revocation, or duplicate execution after uncertain admission. Stop the product expansion if benefits depend on intensive operator coaching, ongoing profile maintenance, or a handicapped native baseline.

The architecture remains general because task definitions, validators, seeds, and authored methods are data and artifacts—not domain switches in production code.

## 8. A minimal interface

Use the conversation as home. Add three focused views, not an organization chart:

**Work:** accepted commitments with current state, last observed evidence, next wake condition, resource position, and exact control scope. Group by user purpose. Workers appear only on expansion.

**Proposals:** possible objectives or method changes, each explaining why it was proposed, what permission it needs, and what happens if ignored. Ignoring a proposal grants nothing.

**Profile changes:** active version, candidate version, plain-language behavioral difference, supporting corrections, scope, evaluation status, activation boundary, and rollback. Exact diffs and digests remain available underneath.

Reuse existing interactions for questions and approvals, `/feedback` for explicit judgments, `/profile learn` for candidate review, `/ask` for separate evidence analysis, and `/fork` for honest copy semantics.  

A correction should allow “this result,” “this engagement,” or “future relevant work,” rather than silently generalizing.

Routine reversible work proceeds within a standing grant. Reserve interruptions for material scope, authority, resource, or irreversible-effect changes. Never replace evidence with “your agent is growing.”

## 9. Sequenced plan, bigger bets, and what to decline

| Milestone | Consequential decision and owner | Pass condition |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Prove useful interpretation** | Braid + seed content; freeze configuration expansion | Users can clarify a need, accept or reject a proposal, correct scope, and finish with conversation alone |
| **2. Prove bounded continuity** | Runtime + existing scheduling owner + providers + Braid adapter | Deterministic failures and the real-cloud lifecycle proof pass against one compatible package cohort |
| **3. Prove worthwhile evolution** | Interface canonical revisions + Eval evidence + explicit user activation | Held-out benefit over the original seed; no loss of authority, provenance, or context separation |
| **4. Decide whether to continue** | Product decision | Adoption comparison beats the native substitute after all user and operating costs |

Remove the proposed requirement that every successful interaction author a child or root revision. Across the experiment, demonstrate a useful exact child profile and a useful non-agent capability **where warranted**. Do not require either everywhere.

Capability creation should face a simple test: does expected future benefit exceed creation, verification, integration, and maintenance costs? A disposable script may be better than a durable specialist.

### Rank the larger bets by evidence this product could earn

**First: persistent personal work assistants.** The experiment can establish continuity, correction, and bounded follow-through. It cannot establish universal personal support.

**Second: reusable verified methods.** Repeated, held-out success can justify reuse within a stated scope. “Verified” must name tasks, versions, assumptions, and failure conditions.

**Third: continuing services.** These require reliable events, exception handling, operational ownership, and service-specific validation beyond chat continuity.

**Fourth: research organizations.** They require shared authority, independent evaluation, institutional memory, and actual research outcomes. Personal ownership and deletion can conflict with organizational records.

**Fifth: external specialist procurement.** This introduces qualification checks, confidential disclosure, payment, disputes, and responsibility for acceptance. The initial product earns almost no evidence for a marketplace.

Do not assume more coordination helps: Google’s controlled study found sharply task-dependent gains and losses, not a universal advantage from adding agents. 

**Decline initially:** autonomous trading or professional services, model training as routine personalization, large fleets, automatic global profile activation, a new scheduler, an occupational marketplace, and cross-harness routing presented as measured intelligence.

No further user clarification is needed for this recommendation. I assume individual ownership, bounded authority, and stop conditions that genuinely bind.

**What would reverse the judgment?** Expand only after users need materially less supervision, corrections remain understandable, recovery survives faults, and methods improve on held-out work. Kill the evolving-root expansion if ordinary native memory and workflows perform equally well with less effort. In that outcome, preserve useful Braid portability and inspection—or narrow further to upstream integration—but do not preserve the larger vision merely because its machinery can be built.
